## The challenge

The housing authority knew that most evictions and shelter entries were preceded by warning signs: missed rent, a utility shutoff notice, a sudden drop in income. The problem was that those signals lived in different places. Rental assistance, social services, utilities support and the shelter system each kept their own records, and no one had a full picture of a household until it was already in crisis.

Caseworkers felt the gap every day. They juggled several systems, re-entered the same details and often learned about a family's situation only when someone walked through the door with an eviction notice. Agency leaders wanted to work together, but they were rightly cautious about privacy, consent and how shared data might be used.

## Our approach

We treated trust as the first deliverable. Before any data moved, we worked with legal, privacy and community representatives to agree on what would be shared, why and with whom. Then we built the tools step by step, testing each one with caseworkers.

- **A clear data-sharing agreement.** Four agencies signed a single framework covering purpose, consent, retention and access, reviewed by residents and advocates.
- **A privacy-first data hub.** We joined records using secure matching and role-based access, so staff see only what they need for their work, through our [data analytics](/services/data-analytics/) practice.
- **Early-warning signals.** Simple, explainable indicators flag households showing several risk factors at once. Every flag is reviewed by a person, and no automated decisions are made about benefits.
- **Caseworker tools and training.** A single case view replaced several systems, and our [change enablement](/services/change-enablement/) team trained more than 300 caseworkers and supervisors on new ways of working.

## The outcome

In the first year, the authority reached twice as many households before they faced an eviction filing or entered shelter. Caseworkers now start their week with a short list of families to contact, along with the reasons each one was flagged, and they spend less time chasing information across offices.

The partnership has also changed how the agencies work together. Monthly reviews bring program leads, data stewards and community voices into the same room to check the signals for fairness and adjust them when needed. The authority is now exploring how the same approach could support older residents at risk of isolation, using the *same agreement and safeguards* rather than starting from scratch.
