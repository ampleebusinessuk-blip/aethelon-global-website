## The challenge

The manufacturer sells household products through thousands of retail partners, and it had grown through a series of acquisitions. Each business unit brought its own systems, so sales, supply chain and marketing data sat in a dozen separate stores. Basic questions, like how a promotion affected sales across regions, took analysts days of spreadsheet work to answer.

Leadership wanted to use AI for demand forecasting and pricing, but the data was not ready. Definitions differed between teams, quality was uneven and much of the infrastructure was aging hardware nearing the end of support. The company needed a modern foundation quickly, without pausing the reporting the business relied on every week. It also needed its own people to be able to run and grow that foundation once the project team stepped back, rather than depending on outside help for every change.

## Our approach

We set a firm seven-month goal and worked backward from it. Rather than moving everything at once, we prioritized the data that would support the first forecasting use case, then expanded in planned waves.

- **A shared data model.** Business and technical leads agreed on common definitions for products, customers and sales, so every team counts the same way.
- **A modern cloud platform.** Our [cloud platforms](/services/cloud-platforms/) team built a secure, scalable environment with automated pipelines and cost controls from day one.
- **Quality and governance built in.** Automated checks catch errors at the source, and clear data owners in each business unit are accountable for fixing them.
- **Analytics teams up and running.** Through our [data analytics](/services/data-analytics/) work, we rebuilt core reports on the new platform and coached internal analysts to own them.

## The outcome

The new platform went live on schedule, and 12 legacy data stores have been retired. Analysts now spend about 60% less time pulling and cleaning data for reports, which frees them to work on questions the business actually cares about. Weekly sales reviews use a single set of figures, and regional leaders can explore results on their own instead of waiting for a custom report.

With a trusted foundation in place, the company launched its first machine learning forecast for a pilot product category within two months of go-live. Early results show steadier inventory levels and fewer emergency shipments to stores. The internal data team is now planning the next wave, bringing in *retail partner data* and extending forecasting to the full product range.
