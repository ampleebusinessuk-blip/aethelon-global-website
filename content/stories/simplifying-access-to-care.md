## The challenge

The health network serves more than a million patients through hospitals, clinics and urgent care centers across a wide region. Yet getting an appointment often meant a phone call, a long hold and sometimes a transfer between departments. Each facility had its own website and booking process, and patients were often unsure where to go for the care they needed.

The burden fell hardest on people who already faced barriers, such as older adults, patients with disabilities and those who speak a language other than English. Call center staff were stretched thin, and leaders worried that frustrated patients were delaying care or going elsewhere. Behind the scenes, aging scheduling systems made it hard to show accurate availability online. Any change also had to respect strict privacy rules and work alongside the clinical systems that staff depend on every day.

## Our approach

We worked with patients, clinicians and front-desk staff to understand how people actually look for care. Then we designed a single digital front door and built the technology to support it, releasing features in stages so the network could learn as it went.

- **One place to start.** Through our [customer experience](/services/customer-experience/) work, we created a single entry point where patients can search by symptom, service or location and see their options clearly.
- **Real-time online scheduling.** Patients can book, change or cancel appointments themselves, with availability pulled directly from clinic calendars.
- **Accessibility from the start.** We tested with patients who use screen readers and other assistive tools, and offered content in the region's most common languages.
- **A reliable cloud foundation.** Our [cloud platforms](/services/cloud-platforms/) team connected legacy scheduling systems through secure, scalable services that protect patient privacy.

## The outcome

Within a year of launch, the digital front door was available to more than a million patients, and 45% of eligible appointments are now booked online. Scheduling calls to the contact center dropped by 30%, which shortened hold times for patients who still prefer to call and gave staff more time for complex requests.

Feedback from patients has been encouraging. Many mention how easy it is to find a nearby clinic or reschedule from a phone, and accessibility testing continues with every release. The network now has a modern platform it can build on, and the next phase will add *virtual visits* and simple reminders for preventive care.
