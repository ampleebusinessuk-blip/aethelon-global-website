## The challenge

The manufacturer makes industrial components in 30 plants across three continents. Over the years, each plant had developed its own way of planning. Some relied on detailed spreadsheets, others on the instincts of experienced schedulers. Sales teams shared forecasts by email, and they rarely matched what the plants had committed to build.

The results showed up in the numbers. Some plants carried too much inventory while others ran short, and expedited freight costs kept rising. On-time delivery had slipped below what major customers expected. Leaders knew they needed a single planning rhythm, but past attempts had stalled because plants saw them as headquarters imposing rules without understanding local realities. This time, the company wanted an approach that plant managers would see as useful to them, not just another report to send upward.

## Our approach

We designed the new process with the plants, not for them. We began with five pilot sites that represented the range of products and maturity levels, then refined the approach before rolling it out across the network.

- **A clear monthly cycle.** Through our [operating models](/services/operating-models/) work, we defined a five-step process, from demand review to executive sign-off, with set dates, inputs and decision rights.
- **Roles that make sense locally.** Each plant named a planning lead who represents local constraints, so the central plan reflects what sites can actually deliver.
- **One shared view of demand and supply.** We built common dashboards that combine sales forecasts, capacity and inventory, supported by our [data analytics](/services/data-analytics/) team.
- **Practical coaching.** We sat in on the first planning cycles at every site, helping teams run the meetings and adjust templates based on what worked.

## The outcome

Within a year, all 30 plants were running the same monthly planning process. Forecast accuracy improved by 15%, and on-time delivery rose by eight percentage points. Inventory is now better balanced across the network, and expedited freight costs have come down as fewer orders need last-minute rescue.

The bigger change is in how people work together. Sales and operations leaders now resolve trade-offs in a single monthly meeting rather than through long email chains, and finance uses the same plan for its outlook. The company has trained an internal team to own the process, and it is now adding *scenario planning* so leaders can test the impact of supply disruptions before they happen.
