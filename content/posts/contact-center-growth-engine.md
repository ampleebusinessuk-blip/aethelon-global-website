For decades, the contact center has been managed like a utility. Leaders watch the costs, push down handle times and look for ways to deflect calls altogether. That approach made sense when service was seen as a necessary expense. It makes far less sense today, when the contact center is often the only place where a company has a real, unscripted conversation with its customers.

Every day, your agents hear what customers want, what confuses them, what nearly made them leave and what would make them buy more. Most of that knowledge disappears the moment the call ends. Changing that is one of the most practical growth moves available to many organizations.

## From cost center to insight engine

The shift starts with a change in how leaders think about a customer contact. Instead of asking "how do we make this call shorter?", ask "what did this call tell us, and what can we do with it?"

When service teams are treated as a source of insight, a few things change quickly:

- Product teams learn about friction points weeks before they show up in reviews or churn data
- Marketing hears the language customers actually use, which sharpens messaging
- Operations sees recurring problems that can be fixed upstream, reducing future demand
- Sales gets warm signals about customers who are ready for an upgrade or a new offer

None of this requires a massive transformation. It requires capturing what happens in conversations in a structured way and routing it to the people who can act on it.

> Your contact center already knows what your customers need next. The question is whether anyone else in the business is listening.

## Agent assist: helping people do their best work

AI has made it far easier to support agents in the moment. Agent assist tools can surface relevant knowledge articles, summarize a customer's history, suggest next steps and draft after-call notes automatically. Done well, this frees agents to focus on the customer rather than on the screen.

The key words are *done well*. In our experience, agent assist succeeds when it is designed with agents, not imposed on them. A few practical guidelines:

1. **Start with the moments agents find hardest.** Complex policy questions, emotionally charged calls and multi-system lookups are good candidates.
2. **Keep the agent in control.** Suggestions should be easy to accept, edit or ignore. Agents who feel second-guessed by a tool quickly stop using it.
3. **Close the feedback loop.** Let agents flag bad suggestions, and show them that their feedback changes the system.
4. **Automate the busywork first.** Call summaries and wrap-up notes are low-risk, high-value wins that build trust.

## Intent data: understanding why customers get in touch

One of the most valuable assets a contact center can produce is intent data: a clear, consistent picture of why customers are reaching out. Many organizations rely on agent-selected disposition codes, which tend to be inconsistent and too broad to be useful.

Modern conversation analytics can classify intent more reliably across every contact, whether it arrives by voice, chat or email. Once you know the real reasons people get in touch, you can prioritize fixes, design better self-service and spot opportunities. A spike in questions about a feature, for example, may signal a training gap, a design problem or unmet demand for a premium version.

Connecting this data to your wider [data and analytics](/services/data-analytics/) foundations is what turns it from a service report into a business asset.

## Measuring value beyond handle time

If you keep measuring the contact center only on efficiency, you will keep getting a cost center. Average handle time still matters, but it should sit alongside measures that reflect value. Consider tracking:

- **Resolution on first contact**, which reflects customer effort more honestly than speed
- **Retention after a service interaction**, especially for customers who called to complain or cancel
- **Revenue influenced**, including upgrades, renewals and cross-sell opportunities raised by agents
- **Insights acted on**, a simple count of issues fixed upstream because of service data
- **Agent experience**, since engaged, well-supported agents create better customer outcomes

A common pitfall is introducing sales targets too aggressively. Customers can tell when a service call turns into a pitch. The best results come when agents are trained to recognize genuine needs and given the freedom to help, with revenue following as a natural result of good service.

## Where to begin

You do not have to rebuild your operation to get started. Pick one queue or customer journey, capture intent consistently, introduce one or two agent assist features and agree on a broader set of measures. Share what you learn with product and marketing every month.
