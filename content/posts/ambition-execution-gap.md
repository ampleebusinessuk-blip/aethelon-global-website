Almost every leadership team we talk to has an AI ambition. Many have a strategy deck, a steering committee and a handful of promising pilots. Far fewer can point to AI that is running in production, used every day, and moving a number the business cares about. In our experience, that distance between what leaders want and what their organizations can deliver is not shrinking. It is getting wider.

The reason is not a lack of interest or investment. It is that ambition scales quickly, while the foundations needed to execute scale slowly. A new model can be adopted in a week. The data, skills, ways of working and measurement that make it useful take months of deliberate effort.

## Why ambition is outpacing delivery

AI has become a board-level topic, and that is a good thing. But visibility creates pressure to announce, and announcements tend to run ahead of readiness. Teams are asked to deliver transformation-sized outcomes using pilot-sized resources. Pilots succeed in controlled conditions, then stall when they meet real data, real processes and real people.

Many teams find themselves in what we call "pilot drift": a growing portfolio of experiments, none of which has a clear owner, a path to scale or a definition of success. Each pilot looks reasonable on its own. Together, they consume attention without compounding into capability.

> The organizations pulling ahead are not the ones with the boldest AI vision. They are the ones that treat execution as the strategy.

## The four gaps holding organizations back

When we look closely at stalled AI programs, the same four gaps appear again and again.

1. **The data gap.** Models are only as useful as the data they can reach. Too often, the data needed for a use case is scattered across systems, poorly documented or governed in ways that make it slow to access. The fix is rarely a grand data platform program. It is targeted work to make the specific data behind your priority use cases trustworthy and available.
2. **The skills gap.** Most organizations have a few people who deeply understand AI and many who are curious but unsure. The gap is not just technical. Product owners need to know how to frame AI problems, risk teams need to know what to ask, and frontline staff need to know when to trust an output and when to question it.
3. **The operating model gap.** AI work cuts across functions, but budgets, decision rights and accountability usually do not. Without clear ownership, promising work falls between teams. This is where a deliberate look at your [operating model](/services/operating-models/) pays off: who funds AI, who owns the outcome, and how decisions get made quickly.
4. **The measurement gap.** If you cannot say what success looks like before you start, you will struggle to defend the investment later. Many pilots are judged on whether the model "works" rather than whether the business outcome moved. Measuring the wrong thing makes it easy to scale the wrong ideas and abandon the right ones.

## A 90-day plan to start closing the gap

You do not need to fix everything at once. You need to build momentum on a few things that matter and make the path to scale visible. Here is a plan we have seen work.

**Days 1 to 30: focus.** Review your current portfolio of AI initiatives and be honest about which ones have a real business owner, accessible data and a measurable outcome. Pick two or three to back properly, and pause or stop the rest. Define success for each in business terms, such as hours saved, errors reduced or revenue influenced.

**Days 31 to 60: build the foundations around the use cases.** For each priority use case, close the specific data gaps that block it. Assign a cross-functional team with a single accountable owner. Run short, practical training for the people who will use and oversee the solution, not just the people building it.

**Days 61 to 90: ship and measure.** Put at least one use case into the hands of real users, even in a limited release. Track the outcome you defined on day one. Capture what slowed you down, because those friction points are your roadmap for the next quarter.

A few pitfalls to watch for along the way:

- Choosing use cases because they are impressive rather than because they are valuable
- Treating governance as a final gate instead of a design input
- Declaring victory on a model's accuracy before anyone has used it in their daily work
- Letting the pilot team carry the change alone, without support from leadership

## From ambition to capability

The gap between AI ambition and execution is real, but it is not mysterious. It comes down to a small number of foundations that can be strengthened deliberately. Organizations that close it do so by narrowing their focus, investing in the unglamorous work around data and ownership, and measuring what actually matters.
