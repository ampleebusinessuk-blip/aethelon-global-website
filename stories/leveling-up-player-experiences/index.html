A global game studio

Leveling up player experiences with applied AI

LinkedIn X (Twitter) Email

At a glance

A global game studio used applied AI to personalize onboarding, sharpen matchmaking and help support teams answer players faster, without losing the human touch.

Impact
18%more new players reaching day seven
35%faster support responses
6AI use cases in production
Industry
Media & gaming
Services
AI strategy, Product engineering

The challenge

The studio's flagship multiplayer title had a loyal core audience, but too many new players drifted away in their first week. Onboarding was the same for everyone, whether they were a veteran of the genre or picking up a controller for the first time. Matchmaking data existed, but designers could not easily see why some matches felt lopsided or why certain players quit after a string of losses.

Player support was also under strain. Each content update brought a wave of tickets, many of them repeat questions about accounts, purchases and known bugs. Agents spent hours on answers that already lived in help articles, while urgent cases waited in the same queue. Leadership had a long list of AI ideas from across the business and no clear way to decide which ones were worth the investment.

Our approach

We started with players, not models. Working alongside the studio's design, data and support teams, we mapped the moments where players struggled and ranked AI ideas by player impact, feasibility and risk. Then we built in small, measurable releases.

  • A focused AI roadmap. We narrowed more than 30 proposals to six use cases with clear owners, success measures and guardrails, as part of our AI strategy work.
  • Adaptive onboarding. A lightweight model reads early play signals and adjusts tutorial pacing and suggested modes, so new players get help when they need it and skip what they already know.
  • Matchmaking insights. We gave designers a dashboard that explains match quality in plain terms, flagging patterns like repeated mismatches that tend to come before a player leaves.
  • Support assistant for agents. An assistant drafts replies from approved help content and routes sensitive cases to people first. Agents review every answer before it is sent.

The outcome

Within two quarters, the share of new players still active on day seven rose by 18%, and designers used the matchmaking insights to tune two seasonal events before launch rather than after. Support response times fell by 35%, and agents report spending more of their day on the tricky cases where a human conversation makes the difference.

Just as important, the studio now has a repeatable way to take an AI idea from pitch to production. Six use cases are live, each with monitoring and a named owner, and the internal product engineering teams run the release cycle themselves. The next round of work is already underway, with player safety and accessibility features at the top of the list.

“They helped us pick the AI ideas that actually mattered to players and then built them with our teams, not around them.”

Chief Technology Officer, A global game studio
